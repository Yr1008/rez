Full pass over every page, the shared stylesheet and scripts, and the homepage walked section by section. Companion to the storytelling audit (Aug 17) - this one covers aesthetics, brand consistency, motion, mobile and speed. Every finding names the file or section it lives in.

What is already excellent, in fairness: the hero film and its scroll choreography, the Engagement Intelligence loop visual, the emotional CTA photography, the glass navigation, and the footer waveform. The problem is not the ceiling - it is that the site performs for one screen and then coasts. The fixes below are mostly reuse of patterns the site already owns.

1 · The structural gap: you cannot hear the product anywhere

This is the single reason "voice-world-class" does not translate. No page currently embeds the live demo. KiraVoiceDemo White.dc.html exists as a component, but zero pages import it - the talk-to-Kira orb, the industry scenarios and the three recorded calls in rz/audio are all unreachable from the site. Every section shows imagery about conversation; none produces sound. Competitors in this niche let a buyer press play inside the first screen - we make them book a meeting to hear the one thing we are best at.

P0Restore the live demo as act two of the homepage

The hero's last beat should hand off directly into "now talk to her" - orb, industry tabs, recorded-call fallback. The component is built; this is a re-mount plus a transition.

P0Micro-players wherever we make a claim

A 15-second real call snippet with a live waveform inside the capability cards and each industry hero. "82% resolved without a human" lands differently when the proof is one tap away.

P1A persistent "Hear Kira" affordance

Small waveform pill in the nav next to Request a demo. The site's one repeated invitation becomes listening, not form-filling.

Dependencies to respect: the widget's two known bugs (slow connect with no real connecting state, intermittent mic capture) become more visible the more prominent the demo is - the connect affordance needs a proper progress state as part of this work, and finance/business recorded clips are still missing (keep those tabs disabled, never fall back to another industry's audio).

2 · Homepage, section by section

Herokeep - this is the benchmark

The film, the scrim choreography and the type scale are the best thing on the site. Two gaps: the hero never hands its energy to a product moment (the next act is a diagram, not Kira), and sound is absent from a film about sound - a tasteful sound-on toggle with captions would be the most honest "video-like effect" we can add.

The Kira platformgood bones, static

The channels-converge-on-Kira diagram is the right idea drawn well, but nothing moves: lines do not draw in, no signal travels the wires, "Loop closed" never ticks. This is the cheapest wonder on the page - stroke-dashoffset draw-in plus a traveling pulse, driven by the same scroll engine the hero already uses.

EI Loop + EI Profilespacing problem

Platform, EI Loop and EI Profiles stack three long near-black screens back to back before the first light section. The dark act reads as one endless scroll rather than three ideas. Either intercut one light interlude or theme the EI pair with the warm-paper system so dark is reserved for the platform statement.

Capabilitiesthree cards, three brands

The 82% / 49% / 60+ cards each wear an unrelated texture - red halftone grid, dark nebula, navy dusk - so the row reads as three different companies. Pick one gradient family from the brand orb set and vary only the hue. The pink chips (24/7/365, First-ring pickup) are not a palette color. Numbers should count up on entry, and each card wants a play affordance (see section 1).

Kira, out loudso close

The section literally titled "out loud" is silent. The two phone mockups are static screenshots: the call timer never advances, no message arrives, the "Kira is on the line" waveform is frozen. Animate the phones (timer ticking, one message sliding in, live bars) and scroll-scrub a short real transcript beside them - this becomes the most shareable screen on the site.

Trusted byweakest section on the page

Five small full-color logos at mismatched optical sizes on flat gray, no hover, no story - it reads as an afterthought exactly where enterprise buyers look for reassurance. Fix: single-ink logo treatment (color on hover), optically normalized sizes, and either a slow marquee or one proof chip per logo. Or fold it into the Featured story band and cut the section.

FAQoff-brand headline

"Questions, answered. Still curious? Talk to us anytime." paints words in four colors - a second gradient moment on a screen that does not need one, and the multicolor treatment reads playful against the enterprise tone. One ink headline with a single amber word does the job.

CTA + footerone brand-rule bug

The emotional CTA is strong. In the footer, the giant animated tagline reads "Now we're talking" without the period - the brand rule is explicit that the tagline ends with one. The waveform canvas beneath it is lovely and should be the motif we reuse elsewhere.

3 · Brand consistency: the site has drifted from the toolkit

None of this needs a redesign - it is governance. There are three competing token sources (Brand Toolkit, rz/site.css, the unused tokens/*.css) and they disagree:

Body font

Brand rule: body and subheads in Open Sans. Reality: site.css v53 sets --body to Bricolage, and 12 of 14 pages do not even load Open Sans from Google Fonts (only Newsroom and Insights do). Every paragraph on the site is off-spec. This is the decided rule in the brand doc - fix the variable, add the font load, bump ?v.

Amber accent
toolkit #EFA12Esite --amber #FFB24D- two ambers in production; pick the toolkit value.
Paper neutrals
#FCFBF9#F4F2EDsite --bg #F4F3F0- near-miss neutrals coexist; consolidate on the toolkit set.
Brand gradient

Left to right: toolkit spec, site.css --grad, footer tagline. Plus the FAQ's per-word coloring - at least four recipes where the rule says one gradient, one moment per screen. Standardize on the spec and route every use through --grad.

Two token systems

tokens/*.css (via styles.css) is the token source behind the Brand Toolkit's own guideline cards, while the live site runs on site.css - two parallel systems whose values have drifted apart (the ambers above are one symptom). Align the values, name one canonical source both consume, and keep the design-only files out of the deploy bundle - do not delete them, the toolkit cards depend on them.

The fix is one change, not fifty: a single :root token block (toolkit values), site.css consumes it, every page gets the same font loads, and the ?v bump ships it everywhere at once. After that, brand drift becomes impossible rather than policed.

4 · Motion: one signature move, repeated

Current inventory: the hero choreography, a blur-fade reveal every section reuses, parallax orbs, button glow, inertial scroll, the footer wave. The decay is that after the hero, every single section enters with the identical rvIn fade - motion stops meaning anything. And none of the motion is about sound, which is our medium. The out-of-the-box move is to make the waveform the site's kinetic signature, then spend motion only where it explains something:

The waveform system
  • Waveform pill in nav, live bars while any audio plays anywhere.
  • Section dividers as a thin animated wave (the footer canvas, reused small).
  • Micro-players render real amplitude, not fake bars.
  • Reduced-motion users get the static wave - already the site's pattern.
Motion that explains
  • Platform diagram: lines draw in, a pulse travels channel to Kira to "Loop closed".
  • Stats count up once on entry (82%, 49%, 60+, NHG 24%).
  • Phone mockups run: timer ticks, a message arrives, call bars move.
  • Scroll-scrubbed transcript beside the phones - a real call, typed by scroll.
Connective tissue
  • Vary reveal direction and stagger per section class instead of one global fade.
  • Cross-fade page background tone between the dark and paper acts on scroll.
  • View Transitions API for page-to-page continuity (progressive, zero cost where unsupported).
  • Hero film: sound-on toggle with captions; industry heroes inherit it.

All of the above runs in the current stack - cine.js already owns scroll progress, nwt-wave.js already draws waves, no new dependencies. Budget rule stays: one gradient moment per screen, motion serves comprehension, never decoration for its own sake.

5 · Mobile: a different, lesser site

Under 640px the hero choreography is disabled wholesale: cards are display:none, the stage transform is removed, and the section collapses to a static poster with the heading floor at 17-22px - against 74px on desktop. The mobile visitor gets none of the wonder the desktop site is proudest of. The performance instinct is right; deleting the story is not the only option:

  • A tap-through hero story - the same three beats as the desktop choreography as swipeable cards over the -m.mp4 loop, driven by touch instead of scroll. Cheap, native-feeling, keeps the film.
  • Keep the count-ups, draw-ins and phone animations on mobile - they are transform/opacity work, not layout work; only the blur filter is worth dropping (already done for reveals).
  • Restore hero type scale pride - 17px hero headlines read as an error on a 400px screen; the site.css "Apple-scale" block caps sections at 23-28px which is right, but the hero itself deserves 34-40px.
  • The nav dropdowns vanish under 860px and Industries becomes reachable only through the sheet - fine, but the indbar (industry pill bar) and the sheet duplicate each other on industry pages; one owner per viewport.
  • The live demo must work on mobile - mic-permission flow and the connecting state need design there first, since that is where "just let me hear it" matters most.

6 · Loading speed: the page is blank until React runs

Asset discipline is genuinely good: heroes are preload="none" video with a preloaded poster (1.84 MB desktop, 753 KB mobile swap), the voice widget (528 KB) lazy-loads, images are webp with lazy loading. The structural cost is elsewhere:

1 · Client-side rendering blocks first paint.

Every page is an empty shell until react-dom (129 KB) + react (10 KB) + support.js (59 KB) download, parse and render - on 4G that is the whole first impression. Highest-leverage fix: pre-render each page to static HTML in scripts/prepare-deploy.sh at deploy time so the poster and headline paint immediately and the runtime hydrates after. This one change likely halves LCP.

2 · Fonts block or flash on 13 of 14 pages.

The homepage gets it right (display=optional plus size-adjusted local fallbacks). Every other page uses default swap and none preload the woff2. Standardize the homepage recipe site-wide; subset to latin while at it - and the Open Sans fix in section 3 adds its load everywhere, so do both in one pass.

3 · site.css is 72 KB and growing by append.

It ships every page, blocking render, and carries page-specific blocks (Mayo mobile fixes, hero mirrors, navfx experiments). One cleanup pass: dedupe the homepage's inlined copy of it, strip dead experiments, minify on deploy.

4 · Texture sprawl.

19 grad-*.webp/jpg variants ship in rz/ while the design rule is one gradient system - consolidating the capability/CTA cards onto 3-4 shared textures (section 2) is a design fix that is also a payload fix.

5 · Housekeeping.

An index.html redirect stub is back in the project root - the deploy script strips it, but readme.md still documents it as the live redirect; align both with the _redirects rewrite so nobody re-ships it. HERO-SCROLL-GUIDE.md describes a JPG frame-sequence hero that production no longer uses (heroes are autoplay video now) - stale docs cost future contributors real time. Never touch rz/voice-widget.js or rz/audio/* in any cleanup.

Set a budget and hold it: LCP under 2.5 s on 4G with the hero poster as the LCP element, total JS before interaction under 250 KB, no regression allowed by the motion work above (it is all transform/opacity and canvas the site already pays for).
