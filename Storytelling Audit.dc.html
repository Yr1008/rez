Storytelling audit, Rezonate vs the niche
Internal · storytelling audit · Aug 17, 2026

Our story vs the buyers who read it.

Audit of the site's narrative against how Sierra, PolyAI and the enterprise-agent niche sell, and against the four people who actually sign. Gaps ranked, each with a concrete fix on a named section.

1 · Who is reading, and what each needs to hear

COO / Ops

Capacity without headcount, cost per resolved conversation. Needs one believable number early.

Outcome owner

Patient access, collections, fan service. Paid on completion rates. Needs the mechanism, not adjectives.

Clinical / compliance

CMIO, risk, legal. Needs governance stated as product behavior: approved workflows, escalation rules, logs.

CIO / CISO

Integration effort, auditability, certifications. Skims for SOC 2, ISO, named systems, data handling.

B2B reality: all four read the same homepage before anyone books a demo. The page's job is not to convert one persona - it is to give each one their sentence within two screens of arriving.

2 · The story the site tells today

Homepage beats: cinematic film ("Now we're talking") → live demo (talk to Kira) → Engagement Intelligence loop → profiles + results → The Kira platform → industries → customer stories (NHG 24%, bank repayment) → security → FAQ → CTA.

What already works: the live demo is a real differentiator (almost no competitor lets a buyer talk to the product on the homepage); the EI category now has a visual mechanism (loop → per-person plan → measured uplift); emotional craft is far above the niche's median; approved proof exists (NHG 24%, 73→87% satisfaction, bank +25% repayment, Gartner Cool Vendor, WhatsApp BSP, SOC 2, ISO).

The structural weakness: the first believable number appears four to five screens down. Emotion opens, but evidence arrives late - and committee buyers skim for evidence first.

3 · How the niche leaders sell

Sierra - sells the outcome, not the tech
  • Prices per successful resolution - the pricing model itself is the trust story.
  • Scale proof stated plainly (reach, named verticals) instead of adjectives.
  • Governance sold as product: goals + guardrails, supervision, agent traces.
  • Research credibility (voice benchmark) feeds the enterprise narrative.
PolyAI - one number, then control
  • Leads with a single resolution metric buyers repeat in meetings.
  • "Non-black-box" control story for ops teams; visibility over what the agent says.
  • Reliability folded in (SLA, 24/7, compliance) - managed partner, not an API.
  • Third-party validation (analyst TEI study) and named CCaaS integrations.

Pattern across the niche: number → mechanism → governance → third-party proof → demo. Emotion is seasoning, not structure. We currently run the reverse order.

4 · The gaps, ranked

G1 · No number in the first two screens

Fix: a one-line approved-proof strip directly under the live demo: "24% general-enquiry workload removed at NHG Health · 73→87% user satisfaction · +25% repayment conversion." All three are cleared numbers; placement needs Peiru's sign-off, nothing new needs approval.

G2 · Credentials buried

Gartner Cool Vendor sits on About; WhatsApp BSP in copy; SOC 2/ISO in the footer; Mayo hidden until PR clears. Fix: a quiet credential row (four marks, one line) under the demo or above stories - the same chain every competitor surfaces at the top.

G3 · No persona routing

Industry pages have buyer cards; the homepage speaks to everyone at once. Fix: one line per persona inside the industries band ("Running access, ops, risk or IT - here is your answer"), each anchoring to the industry page's buyer section.

G4 · Proof lacks denominators

Stories give the delta but not volume, timeframe or baseline - which is what a committee needs to defend the buy. Fix: already flagged as needing approved inputs; when they land, add "over N months / N conversations" to each stat. Longer term: a third-party impact study is the strongest single asset we lack.

G5 · Integrations story is prose

Buyers check "does it fit my stack" before price. We say "works with your systems" but never show which. Fix: an approved-names integrations row (EHR, CCaaS, CRM, WhatsApp) - only cleared names, no logos without permission.

G6 · Pricing silence

Sierra made pricing a differentiator; buyers now ask every vendor. We say nothing. Fix: one FAQ answer in approved language (even "per-conversation, scoped in a pilot" beats silence). Needs a business decision first - flagged, not invented.

G7 · Demo reliability is the story

The live call is our sharpest proof - and its two known bugs (slow connect, mic capture) are therefore positioning bugs, not engineering bugs. Fix: prioritize both; until fixed, keep the recorded-call path one tap away.
